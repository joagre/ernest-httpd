# Language feedback

What writing Ernest felt against the principles, a line each, a queue: a line is carried to Ernest's `language_feedback.md` by copy, Ernest's verdict comes as `language_feedback_reply.md` under its `docs/tmp/`, the log here cites Ernest's number, and the line then goes; a line not answered, or not agreed with, stays with the verdict and the reason beneath it. The file goes when it is empty. The lines are numbered from where the first sweep left off; each below was found on 2026-11-02 against the release of that day, building the design's §2, §6 and §7, and each is shown by a probe under the session's scratchpad, named beside it.

5. **A call on a socket or a listener that another process has closed faults, where one waiting at the close answers `Left(Closed)`.** E.18 states both: a close answers the calls it holds waiting with `Left(Closed)`, and a call after the end faults, `callee was closed` or `callee had ended`. So no other process can stop a listener or end a connection without a fault between two calls: in `probe2.ern` an acceptor's `Tcp.accept(listener, 50)` times out, the entry process calls `Tcp.closeListener`, and the next accept faults; `probe.ern` shows the same for `Tcp.read` after another process's `Tcp.close`. The server has the owner close and kills an idle connection, so nothing waits on it. Principle 1: a reader who knows Erlang predicts `{error, closed}` as a value. Ask: a call on a socket or a listener that `close` or `closeListener` ended answers `Left(Closed)`, the fault staying for one killed.

6. **`Os.exit` from a process subscribed to `Os.termination` waits forever.** §8.6 has the end that `Os.exit` begins wait for every subscriber's reply, and E.23 says `exit` does not return, so a subscriber that exits waits for itself: `probe6.ern` subscribes `main` with `Os.termination(Terminated)` and calls `Os.exit(1)`, and the runtime says `the end waits for 1 subscriber: Probe6.main` and waits until a second termination. The server's entry process, a subscriber, therefore ends the program on a listener's early end by a fault, design §2, where an `Os.exit(1)` would read plainly. Principle 1: a reader predicts that `exit` ends the program; principle 3: the hang is invisible at the line. Ask: the end does not wait for the subscriber that called `Os.exit`, as it does not wait for one that has ended; or E.23 states the case and `exit` faults there, naming it.
7. **E.18 does not say how much a socket takes before a write waits.** "`Tcp.write` ... waits while the connection is behind" predicts that a 32 MB write to a reader that reads nothing waits; `probe5.ern` shows it answers `Right(Unit)` in 0.4 seconds, taken whole, and that writes of 1 MB wait only once about 3 MB stand before them. So the write deadline bounds a wait behind earlier writes and not the response under way, and a slow reader holds a whole response in the socket until the idle deadline and the socket's linger. Principle 1. Ask: E.18 states what a write's deadline bounds, that a write is taken whole where nothing waits before it and waits only behind an earlier one, and how much the socket takes.
