10. **A program's process ends at its `Exited`, so another process's write to it faults.** One process may write a program while another waits in a read on it, as on a socket. Once the reader has read `Exited`, though, the program's process has ended, and the other process's write or resize faults `callee had ended`: `exited_probe.ern`. The writer cannot see the moment, since the reader's message comes after it; in the server, keys that come as the program exits fault the session, and the page sees 1011 where 4004 is due. Principle 1: a socket lives after its connection closes, and a reader predicts a program the same; principle 3: a race no program can close. Ask: a program's process lives after its `Exited` until it is killed or its owner dies, a write then answering `Left(Closed)`; or E.23 states the form a program is written in.

11. **A read whose caller has ended is answered all the same, and what it takes is lost.** A reader killed while it waits in `Os.read` leaves its read with the program, which answers it with the next output, or with `Exited`, to no one: `killed_reader_probe.ern`. After an `Exited` so answered the program's process ends, and the next reader's read faults `callee returned without answering`. E.23 says neither. Principle 1. Ask: the program's process drops a read whose caller has ended, or E.23 states that such a read is answered and its output lost; `Tcp.read` may do the same, unprobed, since the shell closes its socket at once.
