## Three points from outside

*2026-11-17.* Ernest's session read five reviews from outside against this repository at `9c588ce`, and sent three points for the plan to weigh (Ernest's log, *The Outside Reviews Read*). Each was read against the design, the log and the installed documents of 0.10.0.

**The stop time.** The unit had `TimeoutStopSec=45`, above the 40 seconds of `timeouts.write`, `timeouts.file` and `shell.hangup`. A node's end also tells each connected peer and waits for its answer, and a peer that has fallen silent holds the end until it is lost, 45 to 75 seconds (report §8.7). The server has such a peer while an operator's shell is attached. A probe showed it: the server run as a node, a shell attached from `ops` and its processes stopped, and `ern stop`. The subscriber answered at once, and the end took 74.5 seconds, to `the peer ops was lost: it fell silent`. Under the unit, systemd would have killed the server at 45.

The kill costs the server nothing, since the sessions have ended and the log is written by then. But §13 promised an orderly stop before the kill, systemd would record a timeout for a stop that did its work, and an operator who reads Ernest's guide predicts a stop time that covers the silent peer. So the time is 120 seconds, and §13's rule and the smoke test's check add the 75 (principle 1). The other way, a comment saying that the telling of a silent peer is cut, kept the surprise. The longer time costs up to 30 seconds more with the site down, on a restart while a stopped shell is attached.

**The hostile inputs.** The reviews ask for tests of partial reads, timeouts, a client that goes, a stop while sessions end, and malformed WebSocket frames. The tests' names and comments were searched, and not their bodies. A stop while sessions end and malformed frames are held, in `Sessions`, `Session`, `Listener` and `ErnestHttpd`, and in `WebSocket`'s eleven refusals. Four sentences were not found: §6's write that fails, §6's write past its deadline, §11's write past its deadline, and §11's connection closed with no `Close` frame. They are a step of the plan, which reads the bodies first.

Three things were weighed and left. A head that arrives in pieces and is then served is `Http.readRequest`'s loop, whose page says the deadline bounds the whole head, so the row is Ernest's. A WebSocket conformance suite drives an echo server, and the shell echoes nothing: it closes with 1008 on a message that is not the page's. A fuzzer over the decoder defends against a frame that faults it, and such a frame ends its sender's connection alone, which the sender can close already.

Ernest's session then searched its own tests, by their names: none holds a head in pieces, nor the deadline over a head that trickles. So nothing held the bound that keeps a client from holding a connection with a byte at a time, of 80 a listener and 16 an address. The step takes a fifth sentence, §6's head that does not end in time under a trickle, since the test here holds only a head that stops dead. A head in pieces that is then served stays Ernest's: its plan's MVP 4.16, item 99, takes both tests (its log, *The Server's Answers*).

**New code under traffic.** The question was what a deploy that ends no live session would take. The answer went to Ernest's session by a message, and nothing is planned here: the decision is Ernest's plan's, its MVP 4.4's item 6. A session, its shell and a connection need nothing, since a process keeps its code until it ends. The listener's socket cannot be given to another process, so a new listener means refused connections in between. The sessions' registry, the session's messages and the access log's writer are shared across the two builds, and are reached only where their types did not change. A deploy with a new `ern`, a new floor or a changed `config.json` is a restart either way. A session lives 300 seconds past its last key and six run at most, so the server's own need is small.


## The hostile inputs' tests

*2026-11-17.* The five sentences of *Three points from outside* each have a regression test, three in `Connection` and two in `Shell`. The tests' bodies were read first, in those modules and in `Listener`, `ErnestHttpd` and `Smoke`, and none held a sentence. The nearest were a head that stops dead, and a write deadline set past the test's time.

**The writes.** A socket's host takes a write whole, whatever its size (E.18), so no one response fails or waits past its deadline. In a probe a 16 MiB write to a client that read nothing ended in 111 ms, and a write to a client already gone answered `Right`. So §6's two tests have a client ask for a 1 MiB file 33 times in one write. A client that closes at once resets the connection at the first response, and a later write fails. A client that reads nothing fills both ends' buffers, 2.7 MB on this machine, and the next write waits out `timeouts.write`.

Each response the host took is told to the log and the listener, which is right, and the tests hold that the one cut short is told to neither. That the deadline bounds a write's wait behind earlier ones, and never the client's reading of a response, was decided with the caps (*Item 6: the deployment*), and stands.

§11's test of the deadline has the session's stand-in tell the shell 129 outputs of 256 KiB, which the client leaves unread. The shell tells `Written` for each one it wrote, closes at the deadline and tells `Detach`.

**The trickle.** A head that never ends comes a byte at a time, each byte given 10 ms, and is answered 408 at the head deadline of 200 ms, some 19 bytes into its 261. Under a deadline begun again at each read the bytes run out first and the test fails, which a scratch copy with such a deadline showed. The test waits on the log's entry and not on the client's socket: a byte written after the connection's close resets the connection, and the write that then fails loses the 408 the client's socket held.

**The client's close.** The client of an attached shell closes with no `Close` frame, and the shell returns, its session told `Detach`.

**Checked.** Six changes to the program, made in a scratch copy, each failed the test meant for it: a failed write told to the log and the listener, each of the two write deadlines a hundred times longer, a head deadline for each read, `Closed` dropped, and no `Detach`. The two modules' tests ran 30 times on a quiet machine and 30 times under 24 busy processes on its 24 cores, with no failure. The five tests cost 12, 219, 203, 208 and 1 ms.

**Two lines for Ernest.** Line 31: a socket closed while its host holds bytes shows the far end its close a second after its end, though the far end took the last byte at once, and E.18 gives no time. The server shows it. A client that began to read a 4 MiB file 300 ms after its request had every byte within a millisecond, and the close 703 ms later. Line 32: a write that answers `Left(Closed)` loses what had arrived unread, where E.18 says only that each read after a close answers `Closed`. What the tests leave out for the two, the close after a write past its deadline and the 408's bytes, is a step of the plan.

**Weighed and left.** E.18 says of a write that timed out that its bytes "may still be sent", and a probe saw one of two such writes sent at the close. The program closes at its first such write, so nothing of it turns on which. ## The sessions' stand-in shell tells `Written`

*2026-11-17.* `Sessions.startsAnew` failed once in `make test`, on a machine other programs loaded to 54 and more: `ready and nothing more` heard where `ready\r\n` was expected. The cause was the tests' stand-in shell, and not the program. A session reads on only once its shell has told it `Written` (design §11, §12), and the stand-in, an adapter to the test's process, told none. Where the terminal gave `ready` in one read and the line's end in the next, the second was never read.

A probe made the failure at will, so no hunt followed: in a scratch copy, a command that prints `ready` and its line's end a second apart failed the test with that text. The stand-in is now a process of its own that tells its session `Written` after each `Output`, as `Session`'s tests' stand-in does, and ends where a shell closes, at `Unavailable` or `Ended`. The probe's command passes with it, and the module's tests ran 30 times with no failure.

No other module's tests hold a stand-in shell that is told `Output` and tells no `Written`. The stand-in now stands in `Session` and in `Sessions`, a test's helper in each, since a test lives in its module and neither exports the other's.

## The stop time at 85 seconds

*2026-11-17.* Ernest's session told that its tests lost a silent peer about 85 seconds after it stopped where more than 1 MB waited for it, not within the 75 of report §8.7, and that it read the unit's 120 seconds as standing against that. By §13's rule they do not stand. The deployed deadlines are 30 and 5 seconds, and 5 more once the configuration has a shell, so 85 needs more than 125.

The number was measured here before the design took it, on `ern 0.10.0`, by a probe of two nodes on the loopback with the peer's processes stopped. With nothing waiting for the peer the node ended 74.9 seconds after the peer's last traffic, in five runs, by SIGTERM and by `ern stop` alike. With 4.7 to 5.4 MB waiting it ended at 84.9 seconds, in five runs. The loss itself is late there: `the peer p was lost: it fell silent` came at 84.9 seconds with an end begun or without one, and the end followed within 0.05 seconds.

So §13's rule adds 85 seconds where it added 75, the unit's `TimeoutStopSec` is 130, its margin of 5 seconds kept, and the smoke test's check holds the sum (principle 1: an operator predicts an orderly stop before the kill). At 120 systemd would have cut an orderly stop where an operator's stopped shell had output waiting. The unit on the machine takes the time when the switch installs it again, which the plan's step now says.

**A program's end that hangs.** The probe showed more. A node whose program ended by `Os.exit` or by its return, output waiting for the silent peer, did not end within 240 seconds, in six runs of six, and ended once the peer ran again. A SIGTERM sent into that wait did not end it, in three runs of three. The server exits by `Os.exit` where a listener ends early (§2). With a stopped shell holding output it would stay, serving nothing, until the unit's stop time kills it. Nothing here goes around it. It is line 33, and Ernest's item 98 is its fix for the hang, in no release yet.

Three smaller things of the probe went to Ernest's session by a message and are no lines, since nothing of the program rests on them. A lost connection stays open and delivers once the peer runs. The "1 MB" of the host's buffer for a peer does not say what the sockets hold beside it. A node is three processes of the operating system and not one.

**No line for the mapped error.** `Either.mapLeft(step, Io.show)` stands at 72 sites of the program and was weighed as a line. It is none: `step |> Either.mapLeft(Io.show)` writes the same in a binding chain with the step first, as a probe showed, built and taken by the formatter. The form is this program's spelling, and no gap of Ernest's. The sites stay as they are written.
